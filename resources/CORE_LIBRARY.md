# Core Library

This is the serious-study shelf: relatively small, deliberately broad, and chosen to build durable foundations. `S` means foundational/exceptional; `A` means strong recommendation.

## Philosophy, wisdom and ethics

| Grade | Work | Author | Year | Type | Difficulty | Why study it |
|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| S | *Apology, Crito, Phaedo* | Plato | c. 4th c. BCE | Primary | Medium | Socratic method, virtue, death, obligation |
| S | *Nicomachean Ethics* | Aristotle | c. 4th c. BCE | Primary | Hard | virtue, flourishing, practical wisdom |
| S | *Discourses* | Epictetus | c. 2nd c. CE | Primary | Medium | most systematic ancient Stoic teaching |
| A | *Meditations* | Marcus Aurelius | c. 2nd c. CE | Primary | Easy/medium | personal Stoic practice; not a systematic textbook |
| S | *The Problems of Philosophy* | Bertrand Russell | 1912 | Philosophy | Easy | compact entry into epistemology/metaphysics |
| S | *Justice: What's the Right Thing to Do?* | Michael Sandel | 2009 | Popular/academic synthesis | Easy | competing ethical frameworks through cases |

## How to use this library

Do not read front-to-back indiscriminately. For each book record: thesis, strongest argument, strongest objection, five durable ideas, one application, one connection to another field, and one question that remains unresolved.
