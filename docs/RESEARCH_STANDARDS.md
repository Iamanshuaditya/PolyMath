# Research & Epistemic Standards

PolyMath is designed to prevent a large knowledge base from becoming a large confidence machine.

## Resource grades

- **S — Foundational / exceptional**: unusually high value, canonical, rigorous, or primary.
- **A — Strong**: excellent and worth serious study.
- **B — Useful**: good supplement, narrower or less rigorous.
- **C — Optional**: interesting but nonessential.
- **D — Weak/questionable/outdated**: keep only when historically useful or for critique.

## Source classes

PRIMARY SOURCE · ACADEMIC · TEXTBOOK · PRACTITIONER · POPULAR SYNTHESIS · BIOGRAPHY · LECTURE · PODCAST · DOCUMENTARY · BLOG · REFERENCE.

## Epistemic labels

### ESTABLISHED
Backed by strong convergent evidence or broad mature scholarship.

### PROBABLE
Substantial evidence, but meaningful uncertainty remains.

### CONTESTED
Qualified experts disagree or evidence is inconsistent.

### SPECULATIVE
Plausible/interesting but evidence is insufficient.

### PHILOSOPHICAL
An argument, interpretation or normative claim rather than an empirical result.

### HISTORICAL INTERPRETATION
A reconstruction from incomplete evidence; reasonable historians may disagree.

### ESOTERIC / METAPHYSICAL
A claim that is not scientifically established by virtue of belonging to a spiritual/occult tradition.

## Evidence hierarchy by domain

### Psychology / neuroscience
Prefer, roughly:
1. preregistered/high-powered replications and strong meta-analyses,
2. systematic reviews,
3. robust multi-study programs,
4. individual peer-reviewed studies,
5. books by researchers,
6. popular summaries,
7. anecdotes/social media.

A meta-analysis is not automatically correct: inspect study quality, publication bias, heterogeneity and construct validity.

### History
Ask:
- Who produced the evidence?
- When relative to the event?
- For which audience and purpose?
- What genre is it?
- What incentives or loyalties might shape it?
- What independent sources/archaeology corroborate it?
- What do modern specialists dispute?

### Business
Classify claims as DATA, THEORY, CASE STUDY, EXPERIENCE, ANECDOTE or EXPERIMENT.

A founder story is evidence that something happened once—not proof of a general causal law.

### Investing
Separate:
- accounting fact,
- valuation assumption,
- historical return,
- forecast,
- risk estimate,
- narrative.

Avoid treating famous-investor authority as a substitute for valuation or evidence.

### Computer science / AI
Prefer:
1. official documentation/specification,
2. original paper,
3. maintained reference implementation,
4. strong university material,
5. reputable technical writing,
6. tutorials/content farms.

For software, always check version/date because details decay quickly.

## Source-verification checklist

Before adding an online resource:
- [ ] URL resolves.
- [ ] Title/author are real.
- [ ] Publication date is verified or omitted.
- [ ] Original source preferred over mirrors/reuploads.
- [ ] Legal/public access.
- [ ] No duplicated excerpt when a full original exists.
- [ ] Resource type and difficulty labeled.
- [ ] Why it matters is specific.
- [ ] Known limitations are recorded.

## Contrarian-reading rule

For high-influence frameworks, deliberately locate the best criticism.

Examples:
- Kahneman/bias research → replication, statistical-power and ecological-rationality critiques.
- Stoicism → criticisms regarding emotion, detachment, social/political obligations.
- Munger mental models → risks of shallow analogy and post-hoc explanation.
- disruption theory → empirical boundary conditions and later critiques.
- military-to-business analogies → institutional and ethical differences.
- evolutionary psychology → distinguish well-supported evolutionary principles from speculative adaptation stories.

## Anti-guru rule

Never justify inclusion with only:
- a billionaire recommends it,
- a famous university teaches it,
- a viral creator says it,
- it is a bestseller.

Ask instead: What problem does it solve? What is the strongest source? What evidence supports it? What would falsify it? What does it leave out?

## Link policy

Preferred order:
1. original author/institution,
2. university,
3. publisher/journal,
4. official archive,
5. public-domain archive,
6. reputable secondary source.

No pirated copyrighted PDFs.

## Maintenance rule

Every resource entry should eventually contain:
`id, domain, title, creator, year, type, grade, difficulty, epistemic_labels, url, verified_date, prerequisites, why, scope, limitations, related_ids`.
