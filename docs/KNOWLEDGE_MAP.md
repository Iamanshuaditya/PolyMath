# Personal Knowledge Map

This map is the architecture of PolyMath. It is intentionally dependency-aware: do not study everything at once.

## Tier A — Thinking foundations

| Domain | Core questions | Foundations | Prerequisites | Priority | Connections |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|
| Logic & argumentation | What follows from what? What makes an argument valid or strong? | deductive/inductive reasoning, fallacies, argument maps | none | 10 | philosophy, law, writing, science |
| Mathematics | How do quantity, structure and change behave? | algebra, functions, calculus basics, discrete math | arithmetic | 10 | physics, CS, economics, statistics |
| Probability & statistics | How should we reason under uncertainty? | probability, conditional probability, Bayes, distributions, estimation, testing | algebra | 10 | science, investing, experiments, AI |
| Scientific method | How do we learn reliably from observation? | measurement, causal inference, experimental design, replication | logic + statistics | 10 | psychology, medicine, business experiments |
| Learning science | How do we acquire durable knowledge and skill? | retrieval, spacing, interleaving, deliberate practice, transfer | basic psychology | 10 | every domain |
| Writing & rhetoric | How do we clarify and communicate thought? | composition, rhetoric, explanation, argument | language | 10 | leadership, sales, philosophy |

| Domain | Key questions | Core concepts | Prerequisites | Priority | Connections |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|

| Political science & law | How is collective power organized and constrained? | states, constitutions, institutions, legitimacy, incentives | history + economics | 8 | business, geopolitics, ethics |

## Tier C — Nature and systems

Biology → evolution → ecology → human behavior.

Physics → mechanics/energy → thermodynamics → information/complexity analogies.

Systems thinking → feedback → nonlinear behavior → networks → complex adaptive systems.

Study chemistry enough to understand matter, energy, biology, materials and scientific reasoning; it is not an optional blind spot in a serious general education.

## Tier D — Civilization, power and strategy

### Ancient-history spine
Mesopotamia → Egypt → Persia → India/China in parallel → Greece → Macedonia/Alexander → Hellenistic world → Roman Republic → Caesar → Augustus → Roman Empire.

### Strategy spine
Thucydides → Sun Tzu → Machiavelli → Clausewitz → modern strategy/game theory.

Always distinguish military analogy from business reality: firms usually operate inside law, repeat games, market institutions and positive-sum exchange; war often involves coercive zero/negative-sum destruction.

### Leader case studies
- Alexander: Philip II, Macedonian institutions, campaigns, logistics, legitimacy, succession.
- Caesar: late Republic, Gallic Wars, coalition politics, civil war, reforms, assassination.
- Napoleon: Revolution, operational art, administration, coalition warfare, Russia, Waterloo, myth vs record.

## Tier E — Markets, organizations and money

Economics → accounting → corporate finance → business models → strategy → capital allocation → investing.

Business track: market discovery → customer research → positioning → offer → distribution → pricing → unit economics → operations → hiring → management → finance → strategy → capital allocation.

Sales track: prospecting → discovery → qualification → diagnosis → presentation → objections → negotiation → closing → account management → enterprise systems.

Marketing track: research → segmentation → positioning → consumer behavior → copy → channels → conversion → retention → experimentation → growth loops.

## Tier F — Computing and AI

Programming → data structures/algorithms → architecture → operating systems + networking + databases → distributed systems + security → system design.

Web specialization: JavaScript → TypeScript → browser/runtime fundamentals → React → server frameworks → databases/auth/caching/queues/realtime → observability/scalability.

AI specialization: linear algebra + probability + calculus → ML → neural networks → transformers → embeddings/retrieval → inference/evaluation → agents/tool use → AI systems/product design.

Graphics specialization: linear algebra → rasterization → WebGL/WebGPU → shaders/materials → meshes/topology/UVs → PBR → Three.js/Blender → procedural/AI-assisted 3D.

## Tier G — Communication, language and culture

English literature, Hindi literature, mythology, art and rhetoric are not decorative. They improve language, historical imagination, analogy, narrative understanding and models of human conflict.

Greek mythology should be studied through Homer, Hesiod and tragedians before modern pop retellings.

Hermeticism should be split into: ancient Hermetica; Renaissance Hermeticism; modern occult movements; New Age reinterpretations. Metaphysical claims are not scientific findings.

## Missing disciplines added to the original map

1. **Logic** — prevents incoherent synthesis.
2. **Mathematics** — prerequisite for probability, economics, physics, CS and ML.
3. **Biology/evolution** — essential for neuroscience, psychology and human behavior.
4. **Physics** — provides disciplined models of systems, energy and constraint.
5. **Research methods/causal inference** — protects against cargo-cult science.
6. **Sociology/anthropology** — individual psychology is insufficient for institutions and culture.
7. **Political science/law** — power without institutions is an incomplete model.
8. **Information theory** — connects communication, computation, statistics and AI.
9. **Linguistics** — strengthens communication and models of language.
10. **Art/aesthetics** — strengthens perception, creativity, design and cultural literacy.

## Learning levels

- L0 Orientation — map vocabulary and questions.
- L1 Foundations — introductory text/course.
- L2 Core competence — canonical textbook + exercises.
- L3 Deep study — primary sources, papers, historiography.
- L4 Synthesis — cross-domain essays and models.
- L5 Application — projects and live decisions.
- L6 Mastery — teach, debate, write original synthesis.

## Rule

A domain is not "completed" because a book was read. Competence requires recall, explanation, criticism, application and transfer.
