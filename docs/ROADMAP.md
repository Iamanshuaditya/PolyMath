# Personal Learning Roadmap

The objective is not consumption. The output of PolyMath is better judgment, deeper understanding, clearer communication, stronger technical ability and cross-disciplinary synthesis.

## 30-day orientation

### Week 1 — Build the learning machine
- Read the Knowledge Map and Curriculum Graph.
- Set up a Markdown/Obsidian knowledge base.
- Learn retrieval practice and spaced repetition.
- Start an argument journal: claim → evidence → counterargument → confidence.
- Complete 3–5 CS50 lectures/problem sets appropriate to current level or test out via projects.

### Week 2 — Uncertainty and reasoning
- Probability foundations: sample spaces, conditional probability, Bayes, expected value.
- Logic: validity, soundness, induction, common argument failures.
- Write: "How should I change my mind when evidence changes?"

### Week 3 — Humans and incentives
- Intro psychology + behavioral science.
- Intro microeconomics: incentives, opportunity cost, marginal thinking, supply/demand.
- Application: analyze one real buying decision using incentives, social proof, identity, uncertainty and opportunity cost.

### Week 4 — History, philosophy and systems
- Ancient Greece orientation.
- Plato *Apology*.
- Intro systems thinking: stocks, flows, feedback, delays.
- Essay: "What does Socratic intellectual humility require in practice?"

## 3-month foundation

Run four parallel lanes, ~8–12 focused hours/week total.

### Lane A — Reasoning
Probability → statistics → scientific method → decision theory.

### Lane B — Humans/civilization
Psychology → ancient Greece/Rome → philosophy → economics.

### Lane C — Building
Algorithms/systems/software engineering based on placement level.

### Lane D — Communication/application
Writing + sales discovery + one real project.

**Required outputs by month 3**
- 12 synthesis essays/notes.
- 300+ retrieval prompts/flashcards, but only for durable concepts.
- 3 applied analyses.
- 1 software project.
- 1 recorded 20-minute teach-back covering a complex topic without notes.

## 6-month curriculum

### Months 1–2
Reasoning foundations + CS foundation + Greek/Roman history + writing.

### Months 3–4
Psychology, economics, systems thinking, sales, databases/networking.

### Months 5–6
Strategy, business fundamentals, finance/accounting, AI foundations, philosophy/ethics.

**Capstone:** analyze one real company from ten lenses: customer, psychology, market, economics, accounting, strategy, operations, technology, systems, ethics.

## 1-year curriculum

### Quarter 1 — Foundations
Logic, probability/statistics, scientific method, writing, ancient history, CS systems.

### Quarter 2 — Human behavior & markets
Psychology, neuroscience basics, microeconomics, sociology/anthropology, sales/marketing.

### Quarter 3 — Organizations & technology
Accounting, finance, management, competitive strategy, distributed systems, AI/ML foundations, UX/HCI.

### Quarter 4 — Power, synthesis & creation
Political institutions, Alexander/Caesar/Napoleon case studies, strategy classics, ethics, investing, one large technical/business capstone.

**Year-end tests**
1. Explain 100 foundational concepts from memory.
2. Defend and attack 12 major claims in writing.
3. Reconstruct one historical controversy from multiple sources.
4. Build a substantial software system.
5. Analyze a business financially and strategically.
6. Conduct and review real sales conversations.
7. Teach a 60-minute interdisciplinary seminar.

## 3-year long-term map

### Year 1 — Literacy and foundations
Acquire working competence across the major map without premature specialization.

### Year 2 — Depth
Choose 3–4 deep tracks, e.g.:
- software/AI,
- business/sales/marketing,
- history/strategy,
- psychology/philosophy.

Move from popular synthesis to textbooks, primary sources and papers.

### Year 3 — Synthesis and original work
- Publish long-form essays.
- Build original tools/businesses.
- Replicate or analyze empirical studies.
- Teach structured courses/workshops.
- Build a personal knowledge graph connecting people, works, arguments, evidence and applications.

## Daily study system

For a 2-hour serious-study day:
- 45 min READ — textbook/primary source.
- 30 min DO — problems/code/case analysis.
- 20 min RECALL — closed-book retrieval.
- 15 min WRITE — synthesis/counterargument.
- 10 min CONNECT — add links to prior knowledge.

For a 4-hour day, add one course lecture and one applied project block rather than doubling passive reading.

## Weekly cycle

- Monday–Thursday: acquire + practice.
- Friday: retrieval + catch-up.
- Saturday: application/project.
- Sunday: synthesis essay + curriculum review.

## Feynman tests

A topic is not learned until you can answer, without jargon:
- What is it?
- Why does it happen/work?
- What is the strongest evidence?
- When does the model fail?
- What does it connect to?
- Can I give a real example and a counterexample?

## Essay curriculum

Write one serious essay every 1–2 weeks. Example prompts:
- Was Alexander great primarily because of his own genius or Philip II's institutional inheritance?
- Did Caesar destroy the Republic or expose a system already failing?
- Where does Stoicism conflict with modern theories of emotion?
- Are cognitive biases irrational, or adaptations to real environments?
- When do military metaphors make business strategy worse?
- Are incentives more reliable than rules?
- Does wealth increase freedom, and under what definition?
- When does optimization destroy robustness?
- What does Bayes add to ordinary common sense?
- When should a software system remain simple rather than distributed?

## Application portfolio

Maintain a public/private portfolio of evidence of competence:
- historical maps and source comparisons,
- business analyses,
- sales-call reviews,
- product/UX critiques,
- forecasting logs with Brier scores,
- software projects,
- AI evaluations,
- 3D/graphics demos,
- essays and explainers.

The portfolio matters more than a tally of books read.
